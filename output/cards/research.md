# Domestic horror research for the editorial card revision

Status: source study for an authorized content revision. This document records human-created fiction and clearly separates source observations from game-design inferences. It is not a claim about actual spirits, a psychological study of audience fear, a record of a live playtest or evidence that the application implements the revised content.

## Scope and evidence

The approved content boundary is the 147-card primary editorial set plus 38 horror replacements. A horror entry replaces its matching primary mission; it is not appended to the playable total. The 97 existing runtime records are comparison material only, not another playable edition to revise or merge. This research does not change the runtime, protocols, import contract, saved assignments or application controls.

The requested setting is a private house or apartment and its permitted adjacent yard, garden or grounds. A source can contain scenes elsewhere; only its domestic material is eligible for adaptation here. A country mansion is a private dwelling in this classification. Public institutions, hotels, hospitals, theatres and general wilderness are not new story settings. The rural house motifs can be rewritten for an apartment; an apartment scene does not require an outdoor equivalent.

The eight literary entries below use the authors' actual texts, not automatically generated catalogue summaries. The three film entries use licensed-publisher descriptions and essays; these support the stated premises and selected observations, not a claim that complete films were watched. Freddie Francis's account is first-person evidence of production and staging. The BBC-hosted script and official character description support the episode entry; the script was inspected for its setting rather than exhaustively compared with the broadcast.

The machine-readable companion is [fiction-sources.json](fiction-sources.json). Its twelve fixed IDs identify inspiration, not commercial-card provenance. Its year field records the conventional work date; `vourdalak` uses the 1839 composition date. That story was written in French by a Russian author; the Russian text inspected is B. M. Markevich's 1884 translation. The French text consulted was published by Andre Lirondelle in Revue des etudes slaves, 1950, pages 14-33. He is the publishing scholar here, not the translator of a French original.

## Literary observations and separate design inferences

### monkeys-paw — W. W. Jacobs, The Monkey's Paw

Source observation: in a family home, a wish is followed by an intolerable loss and then an unseen return at the door. Knocking increases the conflict between recognizing a loved person and fearing what may be admitted. The object itself seems to move. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/12122/pg12122-images.html).

Design inference: stage access, waiting and withdrawal through a quiet repeated hand-made knock and attention to an imaginary threshold. The actor can reveal intention without a moving prop. Do not impersonate a participant's real bereavement or require a locked door.

### turn-screw — Henry James, The Turn of the Screw

Source observation: apparitions occupy a tower, windows, stairs and the grounds of a private estate. A governess regards their attention as a threat to children; the reader's certainty does not always match hers. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/209/209-h/209-h.htm).

Design inference: an actor can watch a place beyond the visitor, react to an absent figure or hold an unexpectedly intimate gaze. Keep this distinct from the existing movement-under-observation mission. A tower, lake, child role or objectively proven possession is unnecessary.

### old-nurse — Elizabeth Gaskell, The Old Nurse's Story

Source observation: the manor's past exclusion returns through a figure at the window and a repeated confrontation. Visible pleading and striking need not produce sound; a ruined organ nevertheless seems to play. [Primary text in Curious, If True](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/24879/pg24879-images.html).

Design inference: silent, visible knocking creates a contradiction one performer can execute. A request to acknowledge an excluded occupant can replace a literal child-in-peril narrative. Do not require exterior exposure, actual instrument effects or reenacted family violence.

### ash-tree — M. R. James, The Ash-tree

Source observation: the adjacent tree and a bedroom are connected by scratching, indistinct movement and a repeating family threat. The apparent explanation of branches touching the glass is explicitly questioned. [Primary text](https://www.gutenbergca.gutenbergcanada.ca/ebooks/jamesmr-ashtree/jamesmr-ashtree-00-h.html).

Design inference: fingers moving across the actor's own clothing can suggest something crawling where it does not belong. The yard motif remains attached to a dwelling. Do not use animals, concealed bodily contact, open windows, tree climbing or fire.

### empty-house — Algernon Blackwood, The Empty House

Source observation: an ordinary-looking vacant town house seems to replay a pursuit. A fleeting terrified figure and differently weighted footsteps make an absent past feel physically near. This refers to the collection's first story, not its other tales. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/14471/pg14471-images.html).

Design inference: repeat a short unfinished domestic action and distinguish two participants through voice or rhythm. A rescuer can alter the repeated interaction rather than solve an unrelated object arrangement. No running, collision or pursuit on stairs is needed.

### beckoning — Oliver Onions, The Beckoning Fair One

Source observation: rented domestic rooms appear to acquire another occupant. An ordinary grooming sound and signs of occupancy precede the writer's increasing withdrawal and exclusion of a living friend. [Primary text in Widdershins](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/14168/pg14168-images.html).

Design inference: grooming an absent person, arranging an empty place or attending to a second unseen resident gives a performer a specific domestic intention. Use pantomime. The erotic and violent components of the original are not required, and the writer's interpretation is not a real-world diagnosis.

### jolly-corner — Henry James, The Jolly Corner

Source observation: a figure in the protagonist's childhood house hides and then uncovers its face. The confrontation concerns an intolerably different version of a familiar identity; another person's response is different. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1190/pg1190-images.html).

Design inference: let ordinary words, facial affect and bodily greeting disagree. This supplies a motive for concealing or restoring identity without another mirror-copying puzzle. No matching twin, facial alteration, aggressive approach or acted collapse is needed.

### vourdalak — A. K. Tolstoy, The Family of the Vourdalak

Source observation: a known relative returns with altered affect, watches through a window and later knocks to obtain an opening. Recognition and pity help the returned dead approach their family. The house includes an explicit gate and yard. [Russian translation and identified 1884 edition](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Семья_вурдалака_(А._К._Толстой;_Маркевич)/1884_(ДО)); [French original publication](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Famille_du_Vourdalak).

Design inference: familiar language with a detached expression can make a request for admission uncanny. Use invented relationships. The text supports people being persuaded to open; it does not establish a universal magical rule that vampires cannot enter without invitation. Biting, kissing, weapons and child roles are unnecessary.

## Film and episode observations

### others — Alejandro Amenabar, The Others

The licensed publisher establishes a remote private manor, a controlled dark household and new servants disturbing the family's account of its world. Philip Horne's accompanying essay examines repression and the film's reversal of domestic certainty; that reading is criticism, not a statement made by every character. [Film description and credits](https://www.criterion.com/films/33636-the-others); [Horne's essay](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/8292-the-others-something-in-this-house); [distributor synopsis](https://www.rialtopictures.com/catalogue/the-others).

Design inference: address an empty place as occupied, or respond to an ordinary room as if it belongs to someone else. These are physical performances. Do not recreate light sensitivity as illness, impose unsafe darkness or depend on a specific twist for solving a card.

### uninvited — Lewis Allen, The Uninvited

The private Windward House supplies rooms associated with sobbing, cold and a mimosa scent, within a story of disputed domestic history. The publisher's essay is the source for those selected sensory motifs; the studio synopsis supports the purchased-house premise. [Film description](https://www.criterion.com/films/27964-the-uninvited); [publisher essay](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2934-the-uninvited-spirits-by-starlight); [studio synopsis](https://www.universalpicturesathome.com/movies/the-uninvited-1944).

Design inference: a performer can listen to a supposed sob or react as if an ordinary room contains a particular presence. Real cooling and scents are not required effects. Do not introduce fragrances, a cliff-edge task or a claim that the computer sensed an environmental change.

### innocents — Jack Clayton, The Innocents

The publisher identifies a deliberately troubling adaptation of The Turn of the Screw. Cinematographer Freddie Francis's first-person account supports the importance of visible figures, the house/grounds and the treatment of daylight; it is not a complete plot transcript. [Film description and credits](https://www.criterion.com/films/28569-the-innocents); [Francis's account](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3315-freddie-francis-on-the-innocents).

Design inference: threatening attention need not wait for darkness. A still visible person looking past the visitor works in a private garden or room without film editing. Use this as a staging reference alongside the novella, not as a second independent proof of the same plot. Omit sexualized child behavior and coercive rescue.

### blink — Steven Moffat, Doctor Who: Blink

The BBC script locates action at the abandoned Wester Drumlins house and garden. The official character description confirms freezing under observation, movement when unobserved and time displacement by touch. The source beings are aliens, not ghosts. [BBC script](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/writersroom/scripts/doctor-who-s3-ep10-blink.pdf); [official character description](https://www.doctorwho.tv/characters/weeping-angels).

Design inference: the user's existing watched/unwatched follower can retain slow bounded movement and immediate freezing. It does not require touching or a test of how long a participant can avoid blinking. Release through its own reflection is an authored game condition, not a verified solution from this episode.

## Content decisions supported by the research

These are authoring decisions, not facts asserted by the works:

- Give each entity an intelligible local intention: enter, be acknowledged, displace a resident, retain an occupant, recover an identity or repeat an unfinished event. Capture and release should be opposing responses to that same intention.
- Prefer a visible contradiction that a human can produce: silent pleading, a familiar phrase with alien affect, attention to empty space, interrupted domestic action or controlled stillness. Name the performer, action and completion moment.
- Do not convert an ordinary shape-sorting task into spirit behavior solely by changing its title. Retain a household prop only when its use expresses the entity's relation to the place or person.
- Separate discovery from theatrical embellishment. A performer can vary pace or expression, but cannot change the written capture/release conditions or invent a hidden required word.
- Preserve the no-helper format. Observable behavior must supply enough information for discovery; neither this research document nor knowing a source's plot is a required player aid.
- Allow free mixing of eligible stories and missions. Stories establish a domestic situation and atmosphere, without mandatory branches, story-specific passwords, dependency on another card or rules that lock missions to one plot.
- Use no paper, printed tokens, drawing sheets or cardboard task props. Permit ordinary already-available nonpaper household objects only when the card names them. Exclude a task whose actual prop or space is unavailable; do not replace it with automatic success.
- Keep fear separate from the harder subset. Greater tension can come from timing, attention and ambiguity while the performed action remains simple. Retain the existing venue/contact prerequisites and bounded movement.

## Source and acceptance limits

The corpus is selective: eight literary works, three films and one television episode. It is not an exhaustive history, a controlled comparison of what frightens all players, or a set of recovered commercial cards. Critical interpretations are marked as such. A film adaptation and its literary source overlap; they should not inflate claims of independent confirmation.

The works contain deaths, violence, jealousy and sometimes sexual themes. Their selected performance motifs do not require literal reenactment or a detailed retelling of those elements. Use fictional adult identities; a real stop, technical request and contact refusal remain outside fiction and can never cause capture. No source authorizes pretending that an actual emergency is part of the game.

No actor is required to levitate, disappear, alter temperature, move furniture without touching it or produce an unsupported optical effect. No computer is described as observing physical events. Lighting, reflection and acoustic tasks require real local suitability, and their outcomes require the participants' actual adjudication.

Editorial coherence can be checked against this evidence and the card texts. Discoverability without extra assistance, actual fear, venue feasibility, audibility, contact comfort and balance still need a live party. The existing group, scoring and first-game trial rules remain owned by the target rules, not by these fictional inspirations.
